Clinical Interpretation

Key Findings

The consensus ranking identifies the features most consistently associated with mortality across diverse statistical and ML methods. Features repeatedly selected across all methods represent the most robust mortality signals in this dataset.

Top-tier features (appearing in top-10 of 3+ methods) are the strongest candidates for clinical risk stratification. These should be prioritized for external validation.

Note on delta/ratio features: Change-from-baseline metrics (e.g., delta CPO) capture physiologic trajectory, which may be more informative than single timepoint measurements. Their prominence in the rankings suggests that trends matter as much as absolute values.

Limitations & Caveats:
  • Sample size is limited (N=128) — feature importance rankings may change with larger cohorts.
  • Missing data imputed with median; features with >50% missing have unreliable importance estimates.
  • Class imbalance (17/128 expired) limits statistical power for rare-but-important signals.
  • These are associational, not causal, relationships. Confounding and collider bias cannot be excluded.
  • External validation on an independent cohort is essential before clinical deployment.
  • Gini importance can be biased toward high-cardinality features; permutation and SHAP are more reliable.
